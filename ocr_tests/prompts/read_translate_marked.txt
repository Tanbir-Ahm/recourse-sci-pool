You read a photographed page written in {language} (it may also hold English, printed form text, stamps and signatures) and translate it into English. Do two steps in one answer.
Step 1, under the heading TRANSCRIPTION: transcribe the text exactly as it appears, in the original script, line by line (for a page with several columns, read each column from top to bottom, left to right, and start each with a line "[COLUMN n]"). Keep every digit exactly as printed or written; do not convert, correct or tidy anything. NEVER guess: if a word or digit is not clearly legible write [unclear] in its place, and put (?) after a reading you are not sure of. Ignore faint writing that shows through from the back of the paper. Do not skip text and do not invent text. If the text is cut off at the edge of the page, stop where it stops; do not finish the sentence.
Marks for what is on the page besides ordinary writing. Use them exactly, in the transcription: ~~word~~ for a word that is crossed out but still legible; [struck: ?] for a crossed-out word you cannot read; ^word^ for a word written above the line or with a caret, placed where it belongs in the line; [stamp over text] right where a stamp, seal or signature covers writing, and put ? in place of any digit or letter that it hides. Never silently drop a crossed-out word, and never merge a correction into the text without these marks.
A note written in the margin, a printed footer or header (a printer's name and order code, a form number, a page number) goes on its own line in the form [margin: ...], [footer: ...] or [header: ...], at the place where it belongs on the page (a header first, a footer last), and it is never joined into a sentence.
Step 2, under the heading TRANSLATION: translate the whole transcription into English faithfully and completely, in the same order, and keep the same marks: a crossed-out word stays ~~word~~ (translated), an inserted word stays ^word^, [stamp over text] and [struck: ?] stay where they are, and [margin: ...], [footer: ...] and [header: ...] lines stay on their own lines. Convert digits to ordinary digits in the English. Do not add anything that is not on the page.
Names: write the names of people and of small places in English letters the way they sound; do not replace a name with one you know from elsewhere.
Meaning: if the writer's words seem to have a letter or a word missing, or do not make sense as written, do NOT write a garbled sentence. Write the most likely meaning and put <doubtful: written 'the original word', likely meant 'what you think'> right after it. Never use [sic] for this: [sic] is only for a printed number or reference that looks like a misprint, kept as printed.
