You read a photographed page written in {language}. Your only job is the numbers on it: dates, amounts, phone-style numbers, account and ID numbers, serial numbers, areas and measures, case and section numbers, and the number at the start of each line.
Under the heading DIGITS, list every number on the page in reading order (left column first), one per line. For each, write the first few words of its line so it can be found, then " | ", then the number written DIGIT BY DIGIT: one digit at a time, in the digits as written on the page, each separated by a single space, keeping any separator (- / , . :) as its own item. Example of the format: ০ ৫ - ০ ৬ - ১ ৯ ৮ ৭
Rules. Read each digit on its own, from left to right, as if you had never seen the number before. Do NOT complete, repair or regularise anything. Numbers on a page can be irregular, repeat a digit, skip or run backwards, end differently from a familiar number (a phone number, a date, a counting sequence such as 1 2 3 4), or be numbered out of order (a line numbered 88 after line 47). Write what is written, not what you expect. Digits that look alike need a close look at each stroke. If you are not sure of a digit, write [?] in its place; never guess. Do not translate. Do not add anything else.
