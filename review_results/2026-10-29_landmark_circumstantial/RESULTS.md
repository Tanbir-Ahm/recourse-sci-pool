# Landmark case fix, first topic: circumstantial evidence (2026-10-29)

Owner's go, 2026-10-29: "Yes, build the landmark case fix." Scope: item 1 of Claude's comparison of the two answers to "Can a person be convicted on circumstantial evidence alone? What must the prosecution prove?" (Recourse 5 of 10, Anrak 8 of 10): a short list of settled landmark cases, handed to the answer model word for word, with the same mechanism as the trial-delay "anchors". NOT part of this go and not done: the BNSS s.249 padding, the "State of AP" label, the deployed-commit check, other topics.

## What was built

- **Two cases, four passages, copied from the Supreme Court's own PDFs.** *Sharad Birdhichand Sarda v. State of Maharashtra* (1984) 4 SCC 116, three judges: the five conditions ("the panchsheel of the proof of a case based on circumstantial evidence"), the prosecution must stand or fall on its own legs and a false defence cannot fill a gap, the benefit of doubt where two views are open, suspicion is not proof. *Hanumant v. The State of Madhya Pradesh* (1952) AIR 1952 SC 343: Mahajan J.'s statement of the rule that the Bench extracts.
- **A topic registry in Recourse `excerpt_anchors.py`**, each topic with its own switch. `ANCHORS` still governs only `trial_delay` (it has been on in production since 2026-10-16). The new topic is under **`LANDMARKS`** (default off), so merging this code changes nothing on a live site until the owner sets the variable.
- **A question test** (`_circumstantial`): which questions hand the excerpts over (below). A civil-setting guard keeps them out of contract, employment, insurance, tenancy, matrimonial and tax questions.
- **Prompt wording and guidance** that fire only when the words they rely on are among the excerpts: the five conditions as a numbered list in the excerpt's order and words, attributed to the Court and to the Bench of three; the three further points only as the excerpts say them; "do not describe the five conditions as a statute"; a paragraph number only as shown; state the rule from the excerpt first and use a wider-search case only for what the excerpts do not say.
- **The wider search no longer hands the same two judgments over a second time**, although the index spells them differently ("Sharad Birdhi Chand Sarda", a garbled "Hanumant v. The State of Madhya Pradesh.Raojibhaithe State of Madhya Pra"): matched by the court-archive document number as well as by name.
- **The label** "(verbatim excerpt, not yet confirmed by our reviewer)" goes after the first mention of each case, as for the trial-delay anchors; the data may list a short form of a name (`aliases`: "Sharad Sarda") that the labeller then looks for.
- **The sources box, the table of authorities and the PDF/Word export** list both cases with citation, paragraphs and the court's link, with no code change (they read `anchor_excerpts`); the table and the two export files are covered by tests, the Streamlit and Next.js pages themselves are not drawn in any test here.
- **`sci_anchors.py`** (research): per-anchor `verify_options` for old printed reports, a reviewer sheet per topic. `anchors/ANCHOR_SHEET_circumstantial_evidence.docx` is the sheet for Mr. Advocate. `anchors/README.md` says how a topic is added.

## What the word-for-word check showed

`python3 sci_anchors.py verify --write --index <index>`: **4 anchors, 23 segments (10 + 2 + 9 + 2), all found in the court's PDF; all 10 "also in" phrases found in the later judgments.** The two old printed reports needed allowances, named in each check record and on the reviewer's sheet: lines holding only a page number, a footnote mark such as "(1)" stuck to a word, a word split by a hyphen at the end of a line (Sarda); page numbers, hyphenation and one footnote line that the page break puts inside a sentence, `(1) (1838) 2 Lew. 227.` (Hanumant). No word is ignored. The two existing trial-delay anchors needed none and re-verified unchanged.

**A loophole was found and closed while testing this.** The first version of `ignore_footnote_marks` removed a stuck bracketed number from the segment as well as from the court's text, so a wrong number such as "section 313(3)" for "313(2)" would have passed. No real segment contains such a bracket, so the verified data was never affected. Now a segment is never altered: the court's text is read once with the marks dropped and once with them kept, and a bracketed number that is part of the segment must match exactly (negative control in `test_sci_anchors.py`).

**Paragraph numbers.** The archive PDF of *Sarda* carries the printed report's page numbers, not the SCC paragraph numbers. The numbers on the page (151, 152 to 154, 159 to 161, 163, 179 and 180) come from eight later Supreme Court judgments in the index that quote these passages with the numbers (the `also_in` records, all found). Hanumant's is "paragraph 10 of the AIR report", from two later judgments. They are borrowed, not read from the judgment's own PDF; the reviewer's sheet says so.

## Which questions hand the excerpts over (the trigger)

All questions were written by Claude, not by a lawyer. The first set was committed (135976c) before any pattern existed; the patterns were written afterwards by the same author, so set 1 is a weak test. Set 2 was written as paraphrases that the first patterns missed; the patterns were then tuned on it. Set 3 was written after the tuning and run once. The independent pool is 284 questions from ten question files of earlier work (the register exam, the ruler, the Act-shelf and JJ/dowry evals) that were not written for this topic. Re-run: `python3 measure_trigger.py` (the numbers below are from the final code, `trigger_measurement_final_code.json`).

| Set | Wanted: handed over | Unwanted: handed over | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, frozen first (written before the patterns) | 42 of 42 (first run and now) | 0 of 48 | weak test: same author, patterns written after |
| 2, paraphrases | **14 of 26 on the first run** (95% interval 36 to 71%), then 24 of 26 after tuning on it | 2 of 34 on the first run (contract and employment questions), then 0 of 34 | tuned on this set; not an estimate |
| 3, written after the tuning, run once | **22 of 25** (88%; 95% interval 70 to 96%) | 0 of 30 | the fairest figure we have |
| Independent pool | not applicable | **0 of 284** | |
| All unwanted together | | **0 of 396** (95% upper bound about 1%) | |

The three misses of set 3 are paraphrases without the usual words: "If the circumstances proved are consistent with both guilt and innocence, what must the court do?", "Do all the circumstances relied on by the prosecution have to be proved independently?", "Can the benefit of doubt be given where the case rests on circumstances alone?". The two misses left in set 2: "Can the accused be convicted if every fact proved is also consistent with someone else having done it?", "Can I be sentenced if all the police have is that I was seen near the place earlier that day?". They were not tuned on, by rule. **Real people write differently from the author of these sets; the true share of real questions that get the excerpts is unknown and is probably lower than 88%.** Hindi and Hinglish questions have not been tried. A question that is about the topic but never uses its words ("my brother was arrested only because of what people say") does not get the excerpts, and then the answer is what it was before.

Ambiguous questions (reported, not scored): "What is the difference between direct and circumstantial evidence?" hands the excerpts over; so do two of the set 2 questions about customs seizure and contempt. For these the excerpts say nothing about the point and the prompt says so.

## What this does NOT show

- **Nothing has been tried with the answer model** (no model key in the sandbox). Whether the answer now sets out the five conditions, keeps to the excerpts and puts the label where it belongs is for the owner's staging test (`DEPLOY_STAGING.md` Part 4ad). The prompt is a request, not a guarantee.
- **No lawyer has read the passages, the descriptions or the paragraph numbers.** They are `machine_checked`: the words are the court's; whether the passage is the right authority, and whether anything has moved since, is for Mr. Advocate (`anchors/ANCHOR_SHEET_circumstantial_evidence.docx`). We have not added later three-judge or larger Bench decisions on the subject.
- **No hallucination rate has been measured.** The excerpts raise the chance that the answer rests on the court's own words; they do not make it accurate or safe.
- The excerpts say nothing about direct evidence, motive, last seen together, recovery or the Evidence Act / Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam; those parts of the Anrak answer would still rest on whatever the wider search brings.
