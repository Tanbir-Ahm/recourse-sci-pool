# The first search results, in plain terms (written 2026-10-09 for the owner to keep)

## The short version

We are testing the search tool the way you test a student: 30 questions, 19 for practice now and 11 sealed in an envelope for a final exam later. So far we have checked only one simple thing: **did the search put the case your file names among its first five answers?** Today's search does that for about 7 questions in 10. Rewriting the questions in legal language did not help. The biggest problem is not the search method but **gaps in the library**: for 3 of the 27 law questions the right, newer case is not in the index, and then the search shows the old, overruled case instead. Nobody has yet judged whether the answers are actually good; that is what the marking by J and S is for.

## What we did

- 30 questions (your file), each tied to a real reported case. 11 are sealed and will be run once, at the end. 19 are for practice.
- Five ways of searching were tried on the 19: today's search (A), the same without repeated passages (B), without the passage-type weights (E), the Recourse site's own method run on our index (S), and two versions that first rewrite the question in legal wording (C, F).
- Cost: about 13 cents of your $9.88.

## What we measured, and what we did not

We checked whether the case named in your file shows up in the first 5 results (like checking whether the book your teacher named is on the first shelf). That tells you nothing about the other four books on the shelf. Whether they are useful is for the human-style marks (Yes / Partly / No / Misleading) from J and S.

## The results

1. **Today's search finds the named case in the first 5 for 10 of 14 questions.** The no-repeats version also 10; without the passage-type weights 9. The site's own method found it for 6 of 14, but it shows only 3 to 6 passages per question, so that is not yet a fair comparison.
2. **Four questions did not show the named case even in the first 10:** cheque jurisdiction (R09), the business partner's cheating complaint (R14), promise of marriage (R20) and the death sentence with no background inquiry (R22). Other cases may answer them well. Only the marks can say.
3. **Asking the question in legal wording first did not help:** on the 9 questions where it was tried, 6 found the named case against 7 for today's search.
4. **The library has gaps, and gaps are dangerous.** For three questions the right case cannot be found because the index does not have it: the Supreme Court order on the new one-third bail rule for old cases (R06), the 2025 ruling on family welfare committees in 498A cases (R15), and the 2020 ruling on the certificate for electronic evidence (R21). Where the newer case is missing, the search shows the older case it replaced: for R21 the overruled 2018 ruling comes first, for R15 the modified 2017 ruling. It is like a library that has the old edition of a textbook but not the new one. This is the worst kind of failure, because the old law looks confident and nothing warns you.
5. **The ruler's 24 older questions look weaker, but the test is crude.** The expected judgment is in the first 5 for about half (12 of 24) and the exact verified paragraph for only 4 of 24. The index now holds many later judgments that quote the famous rule, and they rank above the original. On the first 12 questions the result is about the same as in October on the smaller index (6 of 12 then, 5 of 12 now).

## What these results do NOT mean

- They do not say which search is best. Fourteen questions is small; a difference of one or two is noise.
- They do not say the answers are good or bad: no marks yet.
- They are a best case: the story questions retell the very cases they test, which real clients do not.
- The sealed 11 have not been touched.

## Six things to remember

1. 30 questions: 19 practised, 11 sealed.
2. Key case in the first 5: about 7 in 10 (10 of 14).
3. Rewriting the question did not help.
4. 3 of 27 law questions cannot be answered because the right case is missing from the index; the old case shows instead.
5. Cost so far: about $0.13 of $9.88.
6. Next: J and S mark 50 chats; then the lights per area; then you decide; the sealed run comes last.

## The other 9 of the 19 practice questions (added the same day)

The "about 7 in 10" is **10 of the 14 questions that could be tested**. The 19 practice questions split like this:

| Group | Questions | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Named case in the first five | 10 (R01, R02, R04, R08, R11, R12, R17, R19, R24, R26) | the search did what the file expects |
| Named case not found | 4 (R09, R14, R20, R22) | see below |
| Cannot be tested: the right case is not in the index | 3 (R06, R15, R21) | the old case shows instead |
| Controls: no right answer by design | 2 (R28 divorce, R29 lawyer at interrogation) | to be marked "No" by the reviewers |

**The 4 that missed, and where the named case was lost** (`miss_diagnosis/`, about 0.7 cent):
- **R09, which court for a cheque presented in Delhi:** the search found Bridgestone (the 2015 ruling) but the re-ranker put it 36th of 60 and preferred the older line of cases (Dashrath and before), which the 2015 Act replaced. A currency problem, not a search problem.
- **R14, business partner's cheating complaint:** the named case (Indian Oil v. NEPC) was nowhere near by meaning (rank 2,241 of 236,155 passages) because the everyday words do not look like its legal language. If it is put in front of the re-ranker it comes first. The first five were on-topic quashing cases anyway.
- **R20, promise of marriage:** narrowly missed, 11th. Later cases that restate the rule rank above it. The first five are on-topic. The BNS 69 half of the question has no case law to find.
- **R22, death sentence with no background inquiry:** not among the first 60 candidates, and even if added would land about 10th. The first five were on-topic sentencing cases.

My reading of the first five for R14, R20 and R22 (not marks): the right kind of case, just not the one the file names. The file names one case per question and others can be right. R09 is the real problem.

**Way forward**
