## 4. Answer shape, the BNSS First Schedule and the other-charges table (later the same day)

**Answer shape (`ANSWER_SHAPE`, default off).** The lawyers' note takes one heading per ask of the person's message, lifts its length for "detailed" (about 1,800 words) and shortens it for "briefly" (about 300), writes the Bottom line as three short labelled lines, and gets a code-built checklist "You asked for ... answered under heading N / the material does not show it / not found". Measured by code only: the length tier of 20 messages written before the first run is read as expected (20 of 20, `test_answer_shape.py`); the owner's follow-up is read as two asks (its "give me detailed explainations" is a style word); the prompt carries the block only for two or more asks or a length word, only for the lawyers' voice, only with the switch on, with the Hard rules block unchanged. **Not measured: whether the model follows the new instructions, or writes the three-line Bottom line without garbling it.** The checklist matches headings by number AND a shared content word, so a note that numbers its issues in its own way (the 9 Nov Q2 note) is shown as "not found" for the ask it did not take up.

**BNSS First Schedule, Part I (`schedules/bnss_schedule_I.json`, `build_bnss_schedule1.py`).** Read from the Gazette PDF the library already held (`raw_pdfs/bnss_2023_gazette.pdf`, pages 158 to 188), by the position of each word on the page (the table has no ruling lines). 438 rows, 287 distinct sections, every section a section of the library's BNS, no empty cell, rows in the Schedule's order, 0 rows whose cognizable and bailable columns hold different numbers of entries. Rows I read against the page text: BNS 64(1) rape (cognizable, non-bailable, Court of Session), 70(1) gang rape (same), 77 voyeurism (first conviction bailable, second non-bailable), 115(2) hurt (non-cognizable, bailable), 117(2), 238, 318(4), 336(3), 351(2). **One source, extracted by a program, not read by a person (`machine_extracted`); not checked against a second source (India Code was not tried: one of its hosts did not answer from the sandbox and the notes record an expired certificate since 2 Nov).** A section with several entries keeps all of them in order and the summary field is filled only where every entry agrees. Offences under other laws are not in Part I; Part II (three rows by punishment) is kept; the IT Act's own section 77B governs its offences. `OFFENCE_CLASS` (default off) hands the rows of the BNS offences among an answer's sections to the answer model as material.

**Other-charges table (`CHARGE_MAP`, default off; needs `CASE_FILE`; lawyers' conversations).** Ten patterns written by me from the BNS text (not read by a lawyer) plus a search by meaning that may add at most three BNS offences (a section with a Schedule row, so BNSS 358/234/244 can never stand in for a charge). Three sets of fact texts, each written before it was run (`questions_charge_map*.py`, `run_charge_map_set2.py`):
- set 1 (14 positives, 12 negatives), first run: 10 of 14 positives had every expected section, 1 of 12 negatives wrongly produced a candidate; the misses were word-list gaps ("intimate", "assault", "stood guard") and two declared-hard items;
- the word lists were then widened and an evidence-deleted pattern was given a crime-context word, using sets 1 and 2 (so those two sets are no longer fair): set 1 12 of 14 and 0 of 12, set 2 (written before the widening; first run 7 of 12 and 1 of 8) 12 of 12 and 0 of 8;
- **set 3, new and written before the widened lists were run on it: 8 of 10 positives complete, 0 of 8 negatives wrongly firing.** Its two misses: "sent it to a group chat" (no sharing word "sent") and "others assaulted her sexually" (not "rape" in the words).
All questions are Claude's. The table says it is not complete and has not been read by a lawyer. The model is asked to say, for each section, what it requires, which facts may match, what the prosecution would still have to show, the classification from the Schedule's line and the defence's points; **nothing of that was tried with the model**.

## 5. BNS 45 (abetment) added as its own candidate (owner's question after comparing with another tool's answer: "what about BNS 3(5), abetment (BNS 45)", then "also criminal conspiracy (BNS 61)")
What was already there: the pattern "a person present at a rape" handed over BNS 70 (gang rape), BNS 3 (the whole section; sub-section (5) is the common-intention rule, formerly IPC 34), BNS 61 (criminal conspiracy) and BNS 49 (the punishment of abetment). What was missing: abetment is DEFINED in BNS 45 (instigation, conspiracy, intentional aid) and the table named 45 only inside 49's reason text, so the definition itself never reached the model. Fix: BNS 45 is now its own candidate before 49 (`charge_map.PATTERNS["present_at_rape"]`); it has no Schedule row (it is a definition; the punishment is in 49), so none is invented. BNS 3(5) and 61 needed no change. The three sections resolve from the library (BNS 3: 4,744 characters, 45: 1,155, 49: 1,033, 61: 1,120).
Measured: `test_charge_map.py` 27 checks pass (two counts changed from 8 to 9 candidates, deliberately); the three question sets give the same numbers as before (set 1: 12 of 14 and 0 of 12; set 2: 12 of 12 and 0 of 8; set 3: 8 of 10 and 0 of 8), because none of their expected lists named 45; full suite 147 of 147. Limits: the pattern fires only when the facts say rape AND presence/helping; the same sections are NOT offered for other fact patterns (for example a conspiracy in a cheating case gets BNS 61, but abetment is not offered there); BNS 3(5) is "common intention", not "common object" (the unlawful-assembly concept in BNS 189 is different, and another tool's answer ran the two together). Nothing was tried with the answer model; no lawyer has read the table.

## 6. Abetment and conspiracy for the other fact patterns (owner: "Also add abetment and conspiracy for the other fact patterns")
**What it does (`charge_map.py`, the companion rule).** When one of seven patterns fires (recording, sharing, threat with material, cheating and forgery, hurt with a weapon, stalking, dowry cruelty) AND the facts show more than one person (a word list `_OTHERS`: co-accused, accomplices, associates, gang, "group of", "others", "two men", "along/together with", "and his friends/brother/partner/parents...", "with his friends..."), the candidates gain BNS 61 (conspiracy), BNS 45 (the definition of abetment) and BNS 49 (its punishment), labelled `companion`, after the pattern's own sections. NOT offered for evidence deleted and harbouring: those are about what happens after the offence, and abetment of the earlier offence needs help given in it. It is not added to every pattern unconditionally because three sections (about 3,300 characters) would be handed to the model, and shown in the table, for cases with one person where they cannot apply. "and his wife/husband" is not in the word list (as often the victim's).
**Changes beyond the ask.** (1) BNS 61 left the cheating pattern, where it was offered whether or not another person was in the facts, and now comes only through the companion rule (a single-actor cheating case no longer lists conspiracy). (2) A gap found by set 4 and unrelated to this rule: the weapon word lists did not match plurals ("sticks", "rods", "knives"), so "beat him with sticks" fired no hurt sections; fixed (`rods?`, `sticks?`, `weapons?`, ...).
**Measured, all fact texts written by Claude, not a lawyer.**
- Set 4 (`questions_charge_map_set4.py`, written BEFORE the rule; development set, because the word list was written with its questions in view): baseline 0 of 10 positives complete (the companions missing, plus the plural gap in item 1) and 1 of 6 single-actor negatives offered a conspiracy (the cheating pattern's unconditional 61); after the rule 10 of 10 positives, 0 of 6 single-actor, 0 of 2 after-the-fact, 0 of 3 no-pattern; the 2 declared traps (the victim's brother; the girl's parents) both wrongly fire.
- **Set 5 (`questions_charge_map_set5.py`, written after the rule was frozen, run once, not tuned afterwards): 8 of 12 positives complete, 0 of 16 negatives wrongly offered.** The four misses: "the other accused" (the list has "co-accused" but not "other accused"); "Her colleagues, including my client"; "My mother-in-law, father-in-law and husband" (the in-laws list is only after "and his/her"); and "filmed a woman in a trial room" with "his associates" (no sexual word, so the recording pattern did not fire at all, which is a gap in the pattern, not in the rule). They are known; widening the word list needs a new unseen set, because sets 4 and 5 are now used.
- Sets 1 to 3 give the same numbers as before (12/14 and 0/12, 12/12 and 0/8, 8/10 and 0/8); `test_charge_map.py` 34 checks; full suite 147 of 147.
**Limits.** The rule reads words, not the law: "more than one person" is not an agreement, and the reason text says so; a case whose facts omit the other persons gets none of the three; the list is a starting point for a lawyer, not read by one. Nothing was tried with the answer model. No hallucination rate has been measured.

## 7. The withheld answer of the staging test (owner: "Yes, build 1, 2 and 3 together")
**What happened (`staging-api` log, build `b256de1`, lawyer question about a bank accountant):** the first answer failed `cognizable_bailable_omission`; the rewrite failed `section_mismatch` (BNS 344 claimed cognizable, our table and the BNSS First Schedule say non-cognizable and bailable); the answer was withheld: Rs 22.17, about 3.5 minutes, nothing shown. The sentence the check objected to was not logged, so the cause is unconfirmed.
**Built (`chat_assistant.py`, `test_claim_window.py`, no switch):** (1) the mismatch problems carry the text they objected to (`text`), so `ANSWER_WITHHELD ... detail=` shows it; (2) `_claim_window`: a claim about a section is read from the sentence or line it is mentioned in, plus ONE following sentence or line that carries on about it (begins with a pronoun or a status word, or is an indented sub-line) and names no other section, never more than the old 300 characters; the omission check uses the same window; (3) when the only reason left after the second try is `section_mismatch`, the answer is kept and a note written by code from our table is appended ("Classification check ... BNS section 344: non-cognizable, bailable"; log line `CLASSIFICATION_NOTE_ADDED_BY_CODE`); any other remaining reason still withholds.
**Checked:** 19 hand-written cases (the staging pattern "Section 344 is bailable. The alleged cheating is a cognizable offence." now passes; same sentence, 'It is ...', an indented sub-line, a table row and a bare status sentence still flag a wrong claim), the full path with the model stubbed (kept with the note after two calls; a right answer untouched; another reason still withholds); suite 148 of 148. Nine saved replies of other tools were run through the old and new window: no verdict differed (0 mismatches either way), so there is NO real-answer evidence that the new window flags or misses anything differently; the cases above were written by Claude with the code in view.
**Limits.** The window can miss a wrong claim written in a layout I did not think of (a status in a later paragraph, a table split over lines); a wrong claim then goes unchecked, where the old window might have caught it. The note is a table value for the BNS section only; a section with two conditions lists both values. The cause of the staging failure is still unconfirmed (the withheld text was never seen). Nothing tried with the answer model. No hallucination rate has been measured.

## 8. The first answer covers the offences asked about; breach of trust (owner: "Go", after the comparison of the bank-accountant answers; Recourse branch)
**Why.** The first answer to "What offences is he likely to face?" named only BNS 344 (the search returned one statute), so it built "bail as of right" on a premise the facts contradict (fake loan files, Rs 62 lakh). The charge map ran only on the button. The other tool's answer listed 61, 336, 340, 318, 316, 3(5).
**Built.** (1) `charge_map.asks_offences` reads the ask sentences of a lawyer's message; when one asks which offences, charges or sections apply, `web_chat.engine_case_file(..., audience="lawyer")` hands the engine the case-file facts plus the message's own fact sentences (`charges: {facts, implicit: True}`), so the first answer gets the table's sections with their text and Schedule rows, and the page shows the code-built table under it; the prompt block says "which offences the client may face"; the "What else could he be charged with?" button is no longer offered once the table has come with an answer (it would hand over the same sections). Needs `CHARGE_MAP` and `CASE_FILE` (both default off); layman conversations unchanged. (2) A breach-of-trust pattern (BNS 316, with the Schedule's rows for 316(2) to (5)): a clear verb of misappropriation, or a plain verb (sold, took, transferred ...) together with a sign of entrustment, with a role and a thing; it joins the companion rule (61, 45, 49 when more than one person).
**Measured (all texts Claude's).** Baseline: 316 offered for 0 of 10 breach-of-trust facts; no detector. Set 6 (development): 10 of 10, 0 of 8 negatives; detector 8 of 8, 0 of 8 wrongly. Set 7 (held out, run once): the first narrow word lists gave **1 of 8** breach-of-trust facts; the detector gave **8 of 8 and 0 of 8 wrongly**. The lists were then widened by structure (strong verbs, or plain verbs plus entrustment) using sets 6 and 7: 9 of 10 and 7 of 8. **Set 8 (held out, run once after the widening): 5 of 8 positives, 0 of 8 negatives wrongly offered.** Misses: "in charge of the stock" (no role word), a guardian who "used" a deposit for his "own business", and goods that "disappeared" (no verb). Sets 1 to 5 unchanged. Suite 148 of 148; `test_charge_map.py` 45 checks.
**Limits.** The pattern is a word list; about three in five unseen breach-of-trust fact patterns are found, so it will miss many real ones. The detector reads words, not meaning ("Which provisions ... could be invoked" is found; unusual phrasings will not be). The table is the start of the list: the model is told to add nothing that is not in the material. A first answer gets up to ten sections' text in its prompt: expect a higher cost per lawyer question that asks about offences (measure `COST_QUESTION`). Nothing tried with the answer model; no lawyer has read the pattern table. No hallucination rate has been measured. Not done from my list: the false positive in the classification check ("does not convert Section 344 into a non-bailable offence"), and the bail-analysis depth for a follow-up like the wife's account.
